A. The innovation

Rethinking the absorbent core.

EcoFem uses processed water hyacinth fibres as part of the absorbent structure in a sanitary pad prototype. The concept is being developed to make thoughtful use of a locally available biomass while pursuing product performance, comfort and lower environmental impact.

Development status The product is a prototype under active testing and improvement. It is not presented as medically approved or finally certified.

B. Why water hyacinth?

A difficult plant with material potential.

Water hyacinth is an invasive aquatic plant that can grow rapidly across waterways.

Its stems contain cellulose-rich fibrous material. With appropriate collection, processing and validation, that biomass may offer a useful input for material applications. EcoFem is investigating that potential in the context of menstrual product design.

This is not a claim that untreated plant material is suitable for product use. Controlled fibre processing, performance testing and safety validation are essential parts of development.

Environmental relevance

Responsible removal can support efforts to manage invasive biomass.

Fibrous structure

The plant contains fibres that can be extracted and investigated for absorbent applications.

Local context

Material exploration can respond to locally visible environmental challenges.

Circular potential

Problematic biomass may become an input for a useful product rather than remain waste.

D. Testing & validation

Evidence before claims.

EcoFem prototypes have undergone comparative absorbency testing against existing sanitary pad products. Results guide product improvement; they do not represent final certification.

Further controlled validation, safety review, quality testing and regulatory assessment are part of the development pathway.
